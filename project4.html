Reverse Engineering Tabletop Spiralizer

Deconstruction consumer product to identify manufactureing methods and efficency through systmeatic analysis, ending in design improvement proposals.

Year: 2025 Role: Engineer Duration: 4 weeks Team: Brian Ahn, Kai Groudan and Jocelyn Lafyatis

Overview

This project reverse engineered the OXO Good Grips Tabletop Spiralizer to gain in-depth understanding of the design and manufactuing descisions in an on-market consumer product. The spiralizer suctions to a flat table and cuts vegitables into spaghatti, fettuccine, or ribbon 'noodle' shapes. Through systematic product decomposisiton, component modeling, and function investication, cost-reduction and design improvemtn opprotunites were propsed.

The Challenge

The OXO spiralizer demonstrated suboptimal assembly efficiency despite its market success. The challenge was identifying which design decisions drove up manufacturing costs and proposing practical improvements that maintain functionality while reducing complexity.

Process

Complete disassembly revealed 44 individual components across four subsystems: main body, suction mechanism, slider, and cutting assembly. The team collectively modeled very part through SolidWorks. Patent research illumiated historical develpoment of mechanical vegitable sclicing and design choices. Design for Assembly analysis calcuated inital part efficency at 56.8%, below the 60% ideal, with excessive fastners and difficult suction assemblies identified as primary inefficiencies.

Outcome

The team proposed three redesigns: converting holes to open slots for easer suction assembly, integrating snap fit features to main body compents for part and fasterner eliminations, and elimation of an under utalized 'push arm'. These suggestions cut assembly time and reduce practical minimum parts while mainting spiralizer funcutioanlity.
