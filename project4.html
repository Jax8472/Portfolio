Reverse Engineering The Noodler

Deconstructing consumer product design to identify manufacturing methods and efficiency through systematic analysis, culminating in design improvement proposals.

Year: 2025 Role: Engineer Duration: 4 weeks Team: Brian Ahn, Kai Groudan and Jocelyn Lafyatis

KeyTunes Project Website

Overview

The Tabletop spiralizer, or the Noodler, is a suction-mounted device that cuts vegetables into spaghetti, fettuccine, or ribbon 'noodle' shapes. This project reverse engineered the OXO Good Grips Tabletop Spiralizer to gain in-depth understanding of the design and manufacturing decisions in an in-market consumer product. Through systematic product decomposition, component modeling, and function investigation, I identified cost-reduction and design improvement opportunities.

The Challenge

The OXO spiralizer demonstrated suboptimal assembly efficiency despite market success. The challenge was identifying which design decisions drove up manufacturing costs and proposing practical improvements that maintain functionality while reducing complexity.

Process

Complete disassembly revealed 44 individual components across four subsystems: main body, suction mechanism, slider, and cutting assembly. The team collectively modeled every part through SolidWorks. Patent research illuminated historical development of mechanical vegetable slicing and design choices. Design for Assembly analysis calculated initial part efficiency at 56.8%, below the 60% ideal, with excessive fasteners and difficult suction assemblies identified as primary inefficiencies.

Spiralizer decomposition

Outcome

The team proposed three redesigns: converting holes to open slots for easier suction assembly, integrating snap-fit features to main body components for part and fastener elimination, and eliminating an underutilized 'push arm'. These improvements would cut assembly time and reduce practical minimum parts while maintaining spiralizer functionality.
